A camera's own words

Two small linear maps, trained on a few thousand (caption, frame) pairs of one camera, teach a frozen image-text model the words of that camera.

0.38 → 0.72AUC of the bear cam word begging on 16 new days (0.5 is chance).
0.12 → 0.42R@10 on the Duluth harbor cam, 742 test frames from 2012 to 2026.
0.06 → 0.23R@10 on the Roatán coral cam, 983 test frames.

How it works

A frozen image-text model learned its English from the web. A camera has its own words: bear codes, ship names, the names of places. We do not change the model. We add one linear map for text and one for images, and we train only the two maps on the camera's own (caption, frame) pairs.

This is the full method. Each map is a residual linear layer that starts at zero, so the maps start as the frozen encoder. The maps are a small linear adapter on frozen embeddings, in the family of CLIP-Adapter. They are not a new kind of model.

class TwoMaps(torch.nn.Module):
    """phi(x) = normalize(x + W x + b). W and b start at zero, so training starts at the frozen encoder."""

    def __init__(self, dim):
        super().__init__()
        self.text_map = torch.nn.Linear(dim, dim)
        self.image_map = torch.nn.Linear(dim, dim)
        for layer in (self.text_map, self.image_map):
            torch.nn.init.zeros_(layer.weight)
            torch.nn.init.zeros_(layer.bias)

    def text(self, x):
        return torch.nn.functional.normalize(x + self.text_map(x), dim=-1)

    def image(self, x):
        return torch.nn.functional.normalize(x + self.image_map(x), dim=-1)


def contrastive_loss(text, image, temperature=0.05):
    """text, image: (batch, dim). Row i of text matches row i of image."""
    logits = text @ image.T / temperature
    target = torch.arange(len(text), device=text.device)
    return 0.5 * (torch.nn.functional.cross_entropy(logits, target) + torch.nn.functional.cross_entropy(logits.T, target))

Recipe: AdamW, learning rate 1e-4, weight decay 1e-4, batch 128, 30 epochs. After each epoch, we measure R@10 on the validation pairs and keep the best epoch. We split the pairs by day, never at random. Frames from one day look alike, so a random split puts near copies of the test frames in the training data.

Duluth harbor, 2012 to 2026

For 14 years, the Duluth Harbor Cam blog posted one or two sentences for each ship that came or went. An example: "Polstream Dabie arrived in Duluth this morning to load grain." Each post has a frame from a harbor camera. The posts are a ship log of the harbor. We use 5,077 posts and their frames from a public repository.

Caution: names on the screen. Many frames carry the ship name as text on the image. In 60 random frames, we counted 18 (about 1 in 3) with the name as an overlay. The maps can learn to read this text. The set maps can match a ship name to the patch that shows it. Part of their gain on ship names can come from the overlay. Where it does not work

  • Tower radio and airport cameras. One day at an airport: 123 radio transmissions, paired with camera frames by time. The model was at chance before and after the maps. The radio talks about aircraft that are a few pixels wide in the frame, or not in view.
  • Chat captions. On a kitten cam and an eagle nest cam, viewers chat. Their captions do not tell what the frame shows. R@50 goes only from 0.13 to 0.23 (kitten cam) and from 0.07 to 0.11 (eagle nest).
  • Plain words. "Sleeping" goes from 0.87 to 0.91. The model knew this word, so the maps add little.
  • Words that lose. On the Duluth cam, the two maps lower "coal" from 0.56 to 0.52 and "iron ore pellets" from 0.58 to 0.47. Both gain with the set maps (0.68 and 0.63). "Ice" and "Indiana Harbor" do not beat the frozen model with either.
  • Several bears in view. A nickname picks the correct bear in 59% of the frames (chance 44%).
  • Fixes with no pairs. We also tried to center and whiten the embeddings, with no pairs. This gave nothing. The maps need (caption, frame) pairs.

Use it on your camera

The code has three short files, embed.py, maps.py and evaluate.py, and a fourth file, sets.py, for the maps over phrases and patches. The notebook runs the bear cam and the Duluth cam from start to end, and it has one function for your own pairs.

  1. Split by time or by visit, never at random.
  2. Start with 500 to 5,000 pairs. More pairs give more words.
  3. Query with the words of the people who wrote the captions. The maps follow the writer, not the camera.
  4. Plain words already work. The gain is in the words of the camera.
  5. Look for on-screen text, such as time stamps and labels. The maps will use it.
